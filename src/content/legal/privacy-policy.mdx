## 3. Information specific to each platform

### Light Rider Cloud

Light Rider Cloud is where your account, wallet and billing live. It handles your profile, credit balance and ledger, purchases and subscriptions, credit transfers, invitations and referrals, and sales enquiries, as described in Section 2. Your profile, wallet and payment details are available to every platform so they can sign you in and charge Credits.

### Light Rider Quantum

| Information | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Job records | Job identifier, the backend you chose, number of shots, status, cost in Credits, and when the job was created and finished |
| Circuits and results | The circuit and parameters you submit, and the measurement results returned, kept by our job service so you can view them |
| Reservations | Device, start and end time, price, the service fee applied, Credits charged, any notes you add, and the hardware provider's reservation reference |
| Interface preferences | Whether you have seen product tours or welcome messages, recently run classroom experiments and list view settings, stored in your browser |

To run a job or reservation, we send the technical content needed (the circuit, target device, number of shots and similar parameters) to the third-party operator of that device, such as IQM, Rigetti or IBM Quantum, under Light Rider's credentials. **We do not send your name, email address or account identifier to hardware providers.** Providers may keep technical logs of the jobs they run, so avoid putting personal or confidential information in circuit names, labels or metadata.

### Light Rider Entropy

| Information | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Draw records | The mode and sources requested, number of bytes, cost in Credits, status, any failure reason, whether the request came from the website or the API, the API key prefix used, and timestamps |
| Signed receipts | The receipt for each successful draw, including the contributing sources, the amount delivered, health-test results, a commitment, the signing key's fingerprint and timestamps, linked to your account |
| Browser session history | Your most recent draws, including the bytes, kept only in your browser tab's session storage and deleted when you close the tab. It is never sent to us. |

**We do not keep the random bytes we deliver to you.** They are never saved to our databases, which have no place to hold them, and receipts do not include them. Our entropy service requests raw randomness from sources, some run by third parties (for example, ANU, Cisco Outshift, QiSpace, IQM, Rigetti and public randomness beacons such as NIST's), under Light Rider's credentials, and **we do not send your name, email address or account identifier to source operators.**

### Light Rider PQC

| Information | What happens to it |
| --- | --- |
| Files uploaded to qSearch | Written to temporary storage on our server, scanned, and **deleted as soon as the scan finishes**, whether it succeeds or fails. They are not written to a database. |
| Scan findings | Returned to your browser and **not stored by us**. A download of findings is created in your browser. |
| Application inputs | Keys, messages, files, secrets, secret shares and tokens you enter into the PQC applications are processed in your browser and **are not sent to Light Rider**. Keys and files you download are created on your device. |
| Entropy for key generation | When an application asks for Light Rider entropy, our servers obtain random bytes (for example, from NIST's randomness beacon or Light Rider's quantum backend) and send them to your browser. We do not save them to a database, and the request does not include your keys or other inputs. |
| Speed-test results | Kept in your browser's session storage, together with your device's platform and number of processor cores, until you close the tab. They are not sent to us. |

Uploaded files may contain personal information, such as names or email addresses in code comments. We process it only to perform the scan, and it is deleted with the file. Server logs may record that a scan ran, its size and any error, but not the contents of your files.

## 5. Cookies and similar technologies

Every platform uses only **strictly necessary and functional** cookies and browser storage. We do **not** use advertising cookies, cross-site tracking, tracking pixels or third-party analytics. Because of this, we do not show a cookie consent banner. If we ever introduce non-essential cookies, we will update this Policy and ask for consent where required.

| Name or type | Purpose | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sign-in session cookie (`logto_...`) | Keeps you signed in to a platform; encrypted | Until you sign out or the session ends |
| Single sign-on helper cookies (`lr_silent_sso_...`) | Check whether you are already signed in to another Light Rider platform, and return you to the right page | Seconds to a few minutes |
| Invitation cookie (`lr_invite_token`) | Carries an invitation link through sign-up so the invitation can be credited | 30 minutes |
| Session storage | Interface state such as the tab or view you chose and dismissed banners, plus the Light Rider Entropy session history and Light Rider PQC speed-test results described in Section 3 | Until you close the tab |
| Local storage | Interface preferences, such as whether you have seen a product tour or which programming language you chose in the documentation | Until you clear it |

You can block or delete cookies in your browser settings, but if you block the sign-in cookies the Services will not work. Our fonts are served from our own servers, so loading our pages does not send your information to a font provider. Some pages may display images hosted elsewhere (for example, a profile picture from your sign-in provider or an image URL you supplied); loading those images lets that host see your IP address.

**Global Privacy Control.** We do not sell or share personal information for targeted advertising, so there is nothing to opt out of. We nevertheless treat a Global Privacy Control signal as a valid opt-out request.

You can help protect your account by using a strong, unique password, enabling multi-factor authentication, and keeping your API key secret. Remember that your API key works across every platform.
